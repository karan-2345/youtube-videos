## Description (YouTube)

When you lose weight, the fat doesn't melt away, turn into muscle or get sweated out. Your body breaks it down with the oxygen you breathe in, and it leaves as carbon dioxide and water. About 84% of it is breathed out through your lungs.

So if you lose 10 kg of fat, about 8.4 kg of it leaves as carbon dioxide in your breath, and the other 1.6 kg becomes water, which leaves in sweat, tears, urine and breath. When scientists asked 150 doctors, dietitians and personal trainers where the fat goes, only three got it right.

Breathing faster on purpose doesn't burn more fat (it just makes you dizzy). Your body burns more when it needs more energy, like when you move.

Sources:
• R. Meerman & A. J. Brown, "When somebody loses weight, where does the fat go?", BMJ 2014;349:g7257
• UNSW Sydney press release on the study (16 Dec 2014): the survey of 150 doctors, dietitians and personal trainers
• NPR "Shots" and ScienceDaily coverage (Dec 2014)

The fat molecule in the video is a real one, C55H104O6, atom for atom: 78 oxygen molecules turn it into 55 carbon dioxide and 52 water molecules.
