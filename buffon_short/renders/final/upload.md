## Description (YouTube)

Drop needles exactly as long as your floorboards are wide, and about 64% of them land across a crack. That chance is exactly 2/π, so twice the drops divided by the crossings slowly turns into π. It's called Buffon's needle (1777).

In 1901 the Italian mathematician Mario Lazzarini said he did it with 3,408 drops and got 355/113, which is π to six decimal places. But honest runs that size usually get only one decimal right, and 355/113 is a famous fraction for π found by Zu Chongzhi around AD 480. His 3,408 drops (16 × 213) and 1,808 crossings (16 × 113) are exactly the numbers that land on it.

The 3,000 needles in the video are a real simulation: 1,918 crossings, which gives 3.1283.
