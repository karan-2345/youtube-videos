# Research notes: "Hidden in plain sight" (vertical Short)

Checked 2026-11-26. Each claim: what we will say, the sources behind it, and a verdict.
**USE** = solid, in the script. **USE (worded)** = true only in the careful wording given. **CUT** = myth, disputed
or not confirmable; kept here so it doesn't creep back in.

## In the script

### 1. FedEx: the arrow between the E and the x (USE)
- The arrow is in the negative space between the "E" and the "x". Designed by Lindon Leader (Landor Associates), 1994.
- Leader said the arrow was not the goal at first: it appeared while he tested tightly spaced letters, and he then
  refined it. His words: "a hidden bonus ... either you see it or you don't."
- Sources: [Retail Brew 2022](https://retailbrew.com/stories/2022/10/07/logo-big-or-go-home-how-fedex-s-secret-arrow-was-created-accidentally),
  [CNN Style](https://amp.cnn.com/cnn/style/article/hidden-designs-famous-logos),
  [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/fedex-logo-secret).
- **FLAG 1:** say "found it by accident, then kept it", not "it was a total accident" (he did refine it on purpose).

### 2. Toblerone: the bear in the mountain (USE (worded))
- A standing bear is hidden in the white space of the mountain: Bern, where the bar was made, has a bear on its coat
  of arms ("Bär").
- 2023: the Matterhorn was dropped from the pack (production partly moved to Slovakia, Swiss "Swissness" law); the
  new generic mountain **kept the bear** (NPR, Fortune, AOL). One outlier (Daily Meal) says otherwise.
- Sources: [NPR 2023-03-06](https://www.npr.org/2023/03/06/1161259572/toblerone-matterhorn-packaging-swissness),
  [Fortune 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/03/06/toblerone-forced-change-matterhorn-design-mondelez-swissness-switzerland/),
  [Newly Swissed](https://www.newlyswissed.com/secret-hidden-in-the-toblerone-logo/).
- **FLAG 2:** don't call it "the Matterhorn" in the present tense. Script says "the mountain".

### 3. Statue of Liberty: the broken chains (USE)
- A broken shackle and chain lie at her feet, partly under the robe, and **cannot be seen from the ground** (NPS FAQ
  and "Statue Statistics"). Bartholdi put them there for liberty breaking free.
- Sources: [NPS FAQ](https://www.nps.gov/stli/faqs.htm), [NPS Statue Statistics](https://home.nps.gov/stli/learn/historyculture/statue-statistics.htm),
  [NPS photo, chained foot](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/FB93E2EB-155D-451F-6756B356235428C9).
- **FLAG 3:** "only visible from a helicopter / from above" is widely repeated but not in NPS's words. Script says
  "you can't see them from the ground", and the picture shows them from above without claiming that's the only way.

### 4. Statue of Liberty: the hidden skeleton by Gustave Eiffel (USE)
- The copper skin is very thin, about 2.4 mm (~0.1 in, Britannica); one NPS-linked source says ~1/8 in and that it
  varies (hand-hammered). An iron pylon and truss frame by Gustave Eiffel's firm carries it.
- Sources: [NPS: Alexandre-Gustave Eiffel](https://www.nps.gov/stli/historyculture/alexandre-gustave-eiffel.htm),
  [NPS Constructing Liberty](https://www.nps.gov/museum/exhibits/statue_liberty/constructing_liberty.html),
  [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Statue-of-Liberty), [copper.org](https://archive.copper.org/education/liberty/liberty_design3.php).
- **FLAG 4:** pylon height differs (92 / 96 / 97 ft): not spoken. Skin: say "less than three millimetres" (true for
  2.4 mm, and for 1/8 in = 3.2 mm it isn't), so safer: **"about as thin as two coins"**. Script uses that.

### 5. Eiffel Tower leans away from the sun (USE (worded))
- Official site: heat makes the tower "a few millimetres" taller, and because the sun heats one side, the top leans
  away from the sun. Press figures of 12-15 cm are disputed and **not** used.
- Source: [toureiffel.paris, "Why does the Eiffel Tower change size?"](https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/news/history-and-culture/why-does-eiffel-tower-change-size).
- **FLAG 5:** the "grows 15 cm in summer" line is cut on purpose; only the lean is spoken.

### 6. Washington Monument: the colour line (USE)
- Work stopped in 1854 at about 150 ft (sources: 150 / 152 / 156 ft) for lack of money, the fight over the
  Monument Society and the Civil War; stone-laying restarted in 1879; the new marble came from a different quarry,
  and weathering made the change visible. NPS counts three quarries / three shades.
- Sources: [Mall History (RRCHNM)](https://mallhistory.org/explorations/show/washington-monument-colors),
  [USGS "Building Stones of Our Nation's Capital"](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/stones/stones4.html),
  [History.com](https://www.history.com/news/5-things-you-might-not-know-about-the-washington-monument),
  [NPS online history ch. 3](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/wamo/history/chap3.htm).
- **FLAG 6:** say "about a third of the way up" (150 of 555 ft) and "more than twenty years", not one exact figure.

### 7. Pantheon: lighter stone toward the top (USE)
- The dome's concrete changes from heavy aggregate at the base to light volcanic stone (tuff, then pumice/scoria)
  near the oculus; the shell thins toward the top; the coffers and the open oculus remove more weight. Still the
  largest unreinforced concrete dome, about 1,900 years on (c. 126 AD).
- Sources: [MIT 21H.405 Pantheon notes](https://web.mit.edu/21h.405/www/Pantheon/Pantheon/Dome.html),
  [Designing Buildings Wiki](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Pantheon),
  [Monolithic Dome Institute](https://monolithicdome.com/pantheon-a-temple-to-all-gods).
- **FLAG 7:** base aggregate (travertine/brick vs basalt) and thicknesses vary by source: not spoken. "A third
  lighter at the crown" appears in one blog only: cut. Whether coffers are mainly structural is debated: we say
  they "take out even more weight", which all sources accept.

### 8. Big Ben: old pennies on the pendulum (USE)
- The Great Clock (Big Ben is the bell) is regulated by stacking pre-decimal pennies on the pendulum; each one
  changes the rate by about **0.4 s a day** (UK Parliament; Britannica). In 2009 a £5 crown joined them.
- Sources: [UK Parliament, Great Clock adjustments 2015](https://webtest.parliament.uk/business/news/news-by-year/2015/august/great-clock-adjustments),
  [Smithsonian 2015](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/big-ben-running-fast-180956409/),
  [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/64921).
- **FLAG 8:** the "2.5 s per penny" figure online is wrong; Hackaday's 0.2 s is the outlier. Spoken as
  "less than half a second a day" (no decimals spoken).

### 9. Burj Khalifa: shaped to "confuse the wind" (USE (worded))
- SOM's Y-shaped "buttressed core" (three wings bracing a hexagonal core); the setbacks spiral up the tower so the
  shape changes with height, which breaks up the wind vortices that would rock it.
- Sources: [SOM project page](https://www.som.com/?p=16224), [Wikipedia: Buttressed core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttressed_core),
  [ICC interview with the lead structural engineer](https://www.iccsafe.org/?p=544195).
- **FLAG 9:** "confuse the wind" is the engineers' popular phrase (Bill Baker, SOM, in interviews) but our sources
  here are secondary; the script says engineers "describe it as" confusing the wind. Setback count (24 vs 27) not spoken.
