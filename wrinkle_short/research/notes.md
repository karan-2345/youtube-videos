# Research notes: why fingers wrinkle in water

Checked on 2026-11-22 with web search. The cloud session blocks several publisher sites, so the facts come from
abstracts and press coverage quoted in search results. Re-read the key abstracts on the laptop before STOP 3.

## Facts used in the script

| # | Claim in the script | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "It isn't **just** your skin soaking up water" | Wilder-Smith 2003; Hsieh 2006; Laytin & German 2025 | The outer layer does take up water, but wrinkling needs intact nerves (fact 3). "Just" keeps the hook honest. |
| 2 | Nerves tell the blood vessels to tighten, the fingertip shrinks, and the skin buckles into folds | Wilder-Smith et al. 2003, *Microvascular Research*: the vasoconstrictor cream EMLA gave nearly the same wrinkling and drop in digit blood flow as water immersion, so wrinkling "is mediated by vasoconstriction". Wilder-Smith 2004 review: altered epidermal electrolytes trigger vasomotor (sympathetic) firing, then vasoconstriction | The pulp loses volume and the skin, anchored underneath, buckles. |
| 3 | A finger with a damaged nerve doesn't wrinkle; doctors have used this as a nerve test | Lewis & Pickering (1930s): no wrinkling with median-nerve palsy (cited in Haseleu 2014). Hsieh et al. 2006: replanted fingers didn't wrinkle and their blood flow rose 22.8%, while normal fingers' flow fell 27.6%. Clinical "wrinkle test": "a denervated digit does not exhibit normal skin wrinkling" (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03813758) | |
| 4 | The wrinkles **might** work like tire treads for grip, "although scientists still argue about that" | Changizi et al. 2011 (the tread hypothesis); Kareklas et al. 2013, *Biology Letters*: wet objects moved about 12% faster with wrinkled fingers, with no difference for dry ones. Haseleu et al. 2014, *PLoS ONE*, 40 subjects: no improvement, "could not be reproduced" | FLAG 1: contested, and the script says so. |
| 5 | The folds come back in the same pattern every time, because the blood vessels underneath stay put | Laytin & German 2025, *J. Mech. Behav. Biomed. Mater.* (doi:10.1016/j.jmbbm.2025.106935), Binghamton: two 30-minute soaks at least 24 h apart gave matching wrinkle patterns, explained by the vessels' fixed arrangement | FLAG 2: "every time" covers days apart; German says it's unknown whether the pattern holds over years. |

## FLAGs

1. **The grip benefit is contested** (2013 found it, 2014 couldn't reproduce it). The script says "might" and "scientists
   still argue about that", and the picture labels it "STILL DEBATED".
2. **"Same pattern every time"** was tested with soaks at least a day apart. Nobody knows yet whether it lasts over
   years, and the script doesn't claim it does.
3. **Already covered?** A web search found no Zack D. Films video about wrinkly fingers (not conclusive). Zack D.
   Films has made knuckle-cracking and bruise videos, so those topics were dropped.
