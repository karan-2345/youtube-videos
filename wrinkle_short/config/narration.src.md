# Narration source: why fingers wrinkle in water (vertical Short, 30-40 s)

> Edit this file, then `python build.py narration`. One line = one beat (a paragraph; 0.8 s after it).
> Sentences inside a beat get 0.4 s. `@pause N` adds silence after the previous beat. `@lead N` adds a picture-only
> moment before the next beat. Every number is a {{fact}} token (this script speaks none).
> v1 (2026-11-22). 100 words, 5 beats, about 35 s (speech measured with Kokoro af_heart 0.95: 31.5 s).
> Rules carried over: the hook lands in the first sentence; every sentence starts with a connective or an explicit
> link to the one before; no fragments, colon reveals or mic-drop kickers.
> Spine: it isn't (just) soaking -> nerves tighten the blood vessels, the fingertip shrinks, the skin buckles ->
> proof: a finger with a damaged nerve doesn't wrinkle (the doctors' wrinkle test) -> maybe on purpose, like tire
> treads, but still argued -> the same pattern every time, because the vessels stay put.

## S00 · Not just soaking
@scene S00_Hook
@lead 0.4
When your fingers wrinkle in the bath, it isn't just your skin soaking up water.

## S01 · Nerves and blood vessels
@scene S01_Nerves
Instead, your nerves tell the blood vessels under the skin to tighten, so the fingertip shrinks and its skin buckles into folds.

## S02 · The wrinkle test
@scene S02_Test
And that's why a finger with a damaged nerve doesn't wrinkle, which doctors have even used as a nerve test.

## S03 · Tire treads?
@scene S03_Treads
So the wrinkles might be on purpose, working like tire treads to grip wet things, although scientists still argue about that.

## S04 · The same pattern
@scene S04_Same
And the folds even come back in the same pattern every time, because the blood vessels underneath stay in the same place.
@pause 1.0
