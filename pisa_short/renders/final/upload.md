## Description (YouTube)

The Leaning Tower of Pisa started sinking in 1178, when it was only three floors tall. It stood on soft, wet clay with a foundation just three metres deep, and engineers say that if the builders had kept going, it would have collapsed.

But Pisa was at war with its neighbours, and work stopped for almost a hundred years. All that time the tower's weight slowly squeezed water out of the clay, which made the ground firm enough to carry the rest. When the builders came back they made the new floors taller on one side to hide the lean, so the tower is actually slightly curved, like a banana.

By 1990 it leaned so far that it was closed. Engineers removed soil from under the high side and straightened it by about 45 cm, and it reopened in 2001. The same soft ground has also protected it from at least four strong earthquakes, because it stops the tower swaying in step with the shaking.

## Facts (all checked in `facts.md`)

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Building started in 1173; one side was sinking by the third floor (1178) | Wikipedia; Burland |
| Foundation about 3 m deep, in soft clay | Wikipedia ("a mere three-metre foundation") |
| Work stopped for almost a century (1178 to 1272) while Pisa was at war | Wikipedia (Genoa, Lucca, Florence); Burland notes the stoppages' reasons are not recorded with certainty |
| The pause let the clay consolidate (water squeezed out) and gain strength; without it the tower would have failed | Burland |
| Upper floors built taller on one side, so the tower is curved ("banana shape") | Wikipedia; Bristol / Scientific American |
| Closed in 1990; soil removed from under the north (high) side; straightened by about 45 cm; reopened 2001 | Wikipedia; NOVA; ICE |
| At least four strong earthquakes since 1280, survived thanks to soil-structure interaction | University of Bristol (2018) |
