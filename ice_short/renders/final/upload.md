## Description (YouTube)

In 1806 Frederic Tudor, a young man from Boston, sent a ship full of frozen pond ice to the tropical island of
Martinique. A Boston newspaper joked that it might prove "a slippery speculation", and it did: most of the ice melted
on the way, the island had no ice house to keep the rest, and he lost thousands of dollars. Years of losses followed,
and in 1812 and 1813 he spent time in debtors' prison. But he worked out how to keep ice frozen: big blocks melt far
more slowly than small pieces, so the blocks were cut square, packed tight and buried in sawdust, which the sawmills
threw away. In 1833 his ship the Tuscany carried about 180 tons of ice from Boston to Calcutta, four months and two
crossings of the equator away, and about 100 tons were still frozen when it arrived. Calcutta's residents paid for an
ice house, India became his best market, and he became known as the Ice King.

What's simplified: "everyone laughed" stands for the newspaper's mocking notice and a city that thought him mad. The
Calcutta voyage was 27 years after the first one, rounded to "thirty". The ships are generic merchant ships of the
time, and Tudor is a figure in period dress, not a likeness. The first Calcutta ice house was paid for by a
subscription among the city's residents.
