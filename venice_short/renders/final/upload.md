## Description (YouTube)

Venice is standing on millions of tree trunks hammered into the mud of the lagoon, some of them more than a thousand
years ago. Wood like that should have rotted long ago, but down in the mud there's almost no oxygen, and the fungi that
rot wood can't live without it. Only bacteria still eat the posts, very slowly, while water fills the wood and keeps
its shape. One church alone, Santa Maria della Salute (built from 1631), stands on more than a million posts.

Simplified for a short video: the posts are drawn bigger and fewer than the real ones (real piles were about 1 to
4 m long and 10 to 25 cm thick, about 9 per square metre); the oldest dated timbers are from the 600s, while most
posts under today's buildings are younger; the bacterial decay is real and some piles are badly decayed, which is
why engineers keep an eye on them.
