| PASS | The Great Pyramid was the tallest thing people built for 3,800 years | [H] c. 2560 BC to Lincoln Cathedral's spire (160 m) in 1311: 3870 years; the pyramid was 146.6 m |
| PASS | we thought nobody had written down how they did it, until archaeologists found the diary of a man who hauled its stones | [M] found 2013 at Wadi al-Jarf; the oldest inscribed papyri known, c. 4,500 years old; before them no written record from the builders' own time was known (Herodotus wrote about 2,000 years later). Simplified: 'hauled' = shipped limestone to Giza by boat |
| PASS | His name was Merer | [M] Merer, an inspector, wrote the logbook (Papyrus Jarf A and B) |
| PASS | its 2.3 million blocks | [B] an estimate (NOVA, Smarthistory, National Geographic); average about 2.3-2.5 t |
| PASS | which went up its ramps | [R] ramps are agreed; their shape is still argued over -- the scene labels the ramp 'exact shape unknown' |
| PASS | at about one every few minutes, every day, for about twenty years | [B] 2,300,000 blocks in 20 years = 315 a day: one every 1.9 min over a 10-hour day, 4.6 min round the clock |
| PASS | Most were cut right beside it | [Q] Edwards 2003 / Lehner: most core stone quarried close by |
| PASS | and dragged on wooden sledges, while someone poured water on the sand in front | [S] the Djehutihotep tomb painting shows exactly this (c. 1880 BC, a later dynasty: simplified) |
| PASS | because damp sand stays firm and the pull drops by half | [S] Fall et al., PRL 2014: the right amount of water roughly halves the force; too much is worse again |
| PASS | the white outer stones came from across the Nile | [Q][M] fine white limestone from Tura, on the east bank; Giza is on the west bank |
| PASS | so Merer's forty men sailed them over in boats, along canals dug almost to the pyramid | [M] a crew of about 40; the diary names the harbour basin 'She-Khufu' at the foot of the Giza plateau |
| PASS | they were cut so precisely that some joints are thinner than a credit card | [P] Petrie: mean joint 0.5 mm (1/50 inch) on the surviving casing stones; a card is 0.76 mm. Only a few casing stones survive at the base |
| PASS | so the pyramid shone white, until people took them away to build Cairo | [C] loosened by the 1303 earthquake and carted off to Cairo (1356; more under Muhammad Ali); stripping had begun earlier (simplified) |
| PASS | its builders weren't slaves, because they were paid in bread and beer | [W] rations of bread and beer were the wages (AERA, Heit el-Ghurab bakeries and breweries) |
| PASS | and buried right next to their king | [W] Hawass: workers' tombs 'built beside the king's pyramid' (the cemetery is a few hundred metres south: 'right next to' is simplified) |
| PASS | thousands of people like Merer | [W] workforce estimates 10,000-30,000 |
| PASS | So it wasn't aliens that built it | [A] quarries, tools, ramps, boats, town, tombs and the diary |
