## Description (YouTube)

For 3,800 years the Great Pyramid was the tallest thing people had built, and we thought nobody had written down how they did it. Then in 2013 archaeologists found the oldest written papyri ever discovered, and among them was the logbook of Merer, an inspector whose crew of about 40 men shipped white limestone from the Tura quarries across the Nile to Giza.

The pyramid has about 2.3 million blocks. Most were cut in a quarry right beside it and dragged on wooden sledges, and pouring water on the sand in front of a sledge (shown in an Egyptian tomb painting) makes it about twice as easy to pull. The fine white casing stones came by boat, along canals dug almost to the pyramid, and they fitted so precisely that some joints are about half a millimetre wide. The builders were paid workers, fed with bread and beer, and buried beside the pyramid.

Simplified in the video: the ramp's real shape is still debated (we show a straight one); "every few minutes" assumes about 20 years of work; the 1300s are when most of the casing was carted off to Cairo, though some was taken earlier.
